<p class="prose-text">Nine verses in two classical meters (अनुष्टुभ् and उपजाति) across four chapters codify Garrett Hardin's <em>Living Within Limits</em><sup>1</sup> and the MIT <em>Limits to Growth</em> World3 model<sup>2</sup> alongside the 2025 Planetary Boundaries update.<sup>3</sup> The treatise covers the impossibility of exponential growth in finite systems, carrying capacity with the Malthusian Demostat, the Tragedy of the Commons and the empirical breaching of seven of nine planetary boundaries.</p>

<aside class="chapter-header" aria-label="Section introduction">
  <h2 class="chapter-title">१. वृद्धिमायाप्रकरणम् (The Illusion of Growth)</h2>
  <p class="chapter-keyword">अनुष्टुभ् (Anuṣṭubh) – 8 syllables per quarter</p>
  <p class="chapter-theme"><strong>Theme:</strong> Infinite exponential growth in a finite system is the worldview of fools. Physical matter cannot reproduce; only paper debt grows without limit. Cowboy Economics must yield to Spaceship Ecology.<sup>1</sup></p>
</aside>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 1</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v1">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">असीमितैव वृद्धिर्या सीमितक्षेत्रमण्डले ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">मूर्खाणां दर्शनं तत्स्याद् अर्थशास्त्रं न तत्त्वतः ॥ १ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">Unlimited growth in a limited sphere – that would be the worldview of fools; it is not true economics.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** असीमिता एव वृद्धिः या सीमित-क्षेत्र-मण्डले । मूर्खाणां दर्शनं तत् स्याद् अर्थ-शास्त्रं न तत्त्वतः ॥

**अन्वयः:** सीमितक्षेत्रमण्डले या असीमिता वृद्धिः (अस्ति इति मन्यते), तत् मूर्खाणां दर्शनं स्याद्, तत्त्वतः (तत्) अर्थशास्त्रं न एव ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **असीमिता** – unlimited. न सीमिता (नञ् तत्पुरुष). The suffix इत from सि (to bind) + क्त.
- **वृद्धिः** – growth / exponential growth. From वृध् + क्तिन् (feminine abstract noun). Represents गुणोत्तरवृद्धि (exponential growth).
- **सीमितक्षेत्रमण्डले** – in the limited sphere. सीमितं च तत् क्षेत्रं (कर्मधारय), तस्य मण्डलम् (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष), तस्मिन् (सप्तमी एकवचन).
- **मूर्खाणाम्** – of fools.
- **दर्शनम्** – philosophy / worldview.
- **स्याद्** – would be. अस् धातु, विधिलिङ्, प्रथमपुरुष, एकवचन.
- **अर्थशास्त्रम्** – economics / wealth-science.
- **तत्त्वतः** – in reality / fundamentally. तत्त्व + तसिल् (adverbial suffix).

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>पूर्वपक्षः · सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पूर्वपक्षः (Opposing View):** The classical economic paradigm, termed "Cowboy Economics" by Kenneth Boulding, presumes infinite reservoirs for both resource extraction and pollution sinks.<sup>1</sup> Throughput is maximized; production is viewed as the creation of wealth from nothingness. This supports the "benign demographic transition" theory, which falsely assumes that wealth can perpetually increase to naturally lower fertility rates without hitting biophysical boundaries.<sup>1</sup>

**सिद्धान्तः (Ecological Conclusion):** The physicist and the ecologist recognize that the Earth operates as a closed system ("Spaceship Ecology" / विमानपारिस्थिनिकी), where the laws of thermodynamics strictly prohibit the perpetual creation of matter and energy.<sup>1</sup> The belief that an economy can maintain exponential growth (गुणोत्तरवृद्धि) indefinitely on a finite planet is philosophically categorized as the worldview of fools (मूर्खाणां दर्शनं), directly echoing Boulding's assertion that "only madmen and economists believe in perpetual exponential growth."<sup>1</sup>

</div>
</details>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 2</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v2">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">द्रव्यं नैव प्रसूयेत ऋणं वर्धेत केवलम् ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">विमानपारिस्थिनिक्यां सत्यमेतद् प्रकीर्तितम् ॥ २ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">Matter does not breed of its own accord; only debt grows without limit. In Spaceship Ecology, this truth is declared.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** द्रव्यं न एव प्रसूयेत ऋणं वर्धेत केवलम् । विमान-पारिस्थिनिक्यां सत्यम् एतद् प्रकीर्तितम् ॥

**अन्वयः:** द्रव्यं न एव प्रसूयेत, केवलम् ऋणं वर्धेत। विमानपारिस्थिनिक्यां एतद् सत्यम् प्रकीर्तितम् ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **द्रव्यम्** – matter / substantive physical wealth.
- **प्रसूयेत** – breeds / reproduces. प्र + सू (to give birth) + विधिलिङ्, आत्मनेपद.
- **ऋणम्** – debt / paper wealth.
- **वर्धेत** – grows (exponentially). वृध् + विधिलिङ्, आत्मनेपद.
- **केवलम्** – only.
- **विमानपारिस्थिनिक्याम्** – in Spaceship Ecology. विमानस्य पारिस्थिनिकी (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष). पारिस्थिनिकी from परि + स्था + इनि + ङीप्.<sup>9</sup> सप्तमी एकवचन. Syllables: vi-mā-na-pā-ris-thi-ni-kyāṃ = 8.
- **सत्यम्** – truth.
- **प्रकीर्तितम्** – is declared.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

The distinction between real physical wealth and illusory paper wealth is mathematically formalized. As हारडिन् demonstrates using the analogy of compound interest, physical matter (such as gold or fossil fuels) cannot organically reproduce itself.<sup>1</sup> If a priest deposits two grams of gold in a bank at 5 percent interest for 2,000 years, the mathematical yield would require 800 trillion solid gold Earths.<sup>1</sup> Only conceptual debt (ऋणम्) can grow exponentially without physical limits on paper. The verse codifies the transition from the open, limitless paradigm to Spaceship Ecology (विमानपारिस्थिनिकी), wherein conservation of mass and energy dictates that all apparent "production" is merely the transformation of existing resources.<sup>1</sup>

</div>
</details>

<aside class="chapter-header" aria-label="Section introduction">
  <h2 class="chapter-title">२. धारणक्षमताप्रकरणम् (Carrying Capacity and the Demostat)</h2>
  <p class="chapter-keyword">अनुष्टुभ् (Anuṣṭubh) – 8 syllables per quarter</p>
  <p class="chapter-theme"><strong>Theme:</strong> When population exceeds carrying capacity, misery acts as the direct regulator – the Malthusian Demostat. True carrying capacity is not bare survival but encompasses five dimensions: food, water, space, energy and solitude.<sup>1</sup></p>
</aside>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 3</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v3">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">मालतुषस्य सूत्रेण क्लेशः साक्षाद् नियामकः ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">धारणक्षमतातीते प्रजानियन्त्रकः स्मृतः ॥ ३ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">By the principle of Malthus, misery is the direct regulator. When carrying capacity is exceeded, it is recognized as the population thermostat.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** मालतुषस्य सूत्रेण क्लेशः साक्षाद् नियामकः । धारण-क्षमता-अतीते प्रजा-नियन्त्रकः स्मृतः ॥

**अन्वयः:** मालतुषस्य सूत्रेण (यदा लोकः) धारणक्षमतातीते (भवति तदा) क्लेशः साक्षाद् नियामकः (भवति, अयम् एव) प्रजानियन्त्रकः स्मृतः ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **मालतुषस्य** – of Malthus. माल (abundance) + तुष (chaff). षष्ठी एकवचन.
- **सूत्रेण** – by the principle / formula.
- **क्लेशः** – misery / suffering.
- **साक्षाद्** – directly.
- **नियामकः** – regulator / controller.
- **धारणक्षमतातीते** – when carrying capacity is exceeded. धारणस्य क्षमता (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष) = धारणक्षमता.<sup>5</sup> धारणक्षमताम् अतीतः (द्वितीया तत्पुरुष). तस्मिन् (सप्तमी एकवचन). Locative absolute.
- **प्रजानियन्त्रकः** – population thermostat (Demostat). प्रजानां नियन्त्रकः (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष). Neologism for Hardin's "Demostat."<sup>1</sup>
- **स्मृतः** – is recognized / remembered.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>पूर्वपक्षः · सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पूर्वपक्षः:** Technological optimists argue that human populations are immune to the biological laws governing animal herds. They assert that every mouth comes with two hands, implying that human ingenuity will perpetually expand the limits of subsistence.<sup>1</sup>

**सिद्धान्तः:** The verse constructs the cybernetic mechanism described by Thomas Robert Malthus and formalized by हारडिन् as the "Malthusian Demostat" (प्रजानियन्त्रक).<sup>1</sup> Malthus observed that human populations exhibit an innate tendency for exponential growth but are ultimately bounded by carrying capacity (धारणक्षमता). When a population exceeds this threshold, a negative feedback loop triggers. Malthus identified this feedback as "misery and vice" (क्लेशः).<sup>1</sup> The verse codifies that misery acts as the direct regulator (साक्षाद् नियामकः), restoring equilibrium by increasing mortality or suppressing fertility until the population falls back within environmental limits.

</div>
</details>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 4</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v4">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">अन्नं जलं तथाकाशम् ऊर्जा चैव चतुर्थिका ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">पञ्चमं मौनमन्विच्छेद् एताः सीमाः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥ ४ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">Food, water, space and the fourth being energy – one should seek the fifth, solitude. These limits are declared.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** अन्नं जलं तथा आकाशम् ऊर्जा च एव चतुर्थिका । पञ्चमं मौनम् अन्विच्छेद् एताः सीमाः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥

**अन्वयः:** अन्नं जलं तथा आकाशम् एव च चतुर्थिका ऊर्जा, पञ्चमं मौनम् अन्विच्छेद्, एताः सीमाः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **अन्नम्** – food / agricultural yield.
- **जलम्** – water / freshwater resources.
- **आकाशम्** – space / standing room / habitat.
- **ऊर्जा** – energy / fossil fuels.
- **चतुर्थिका** – the fourth. चतुर + थ + कन् + टाप्.
- **पञ्चमम्** – the fifth.
- **मौनम्** – silence / solitude / wilderness amenities.
- **अन्विच्छेद्** – one should desire / seek. अनु + इष् (to seek) + विधिलिङ्, प्रथमपुरुष, एकवचन.
- **सीमाः** – limits / boundaries. सीमन्, प्रथमा बहुवचन.
- **प्रकीर्तिताः** – are declared.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

The verse expands the definition of carrying capacity from a purely biological metric (sustenance calories) to "Cultural Carrying Capacity."<sup>1</sup> While a minimum baseline of food (अन्नम्) and water (जलम्) defines the absolute biological limit, human well-being requires space (आकाशम्) and high energy inputs (ऊर्जा) equivalent to roughly 230,000 calories per day for a modern citizen.<sup>1</sup> Psychological and cultural health demands access to amenities such as solitude and untrammeled wilderness (मौनम्). By maximizing sheer numbers, society inevitably minimizes per capita availability of these cultural amenities.<sup>1</sup> The true limit of a prosperous society is dictated not by bare survival but by the preservation of these five boundaries.

</div>
</details>

<aside class="chapter-header" aria-label="Section introduction">
  <h2 class="chapter-title">३. साधारणक्षेत्रविषादप्रकरणम् (The Tragedy of the Commons)</h2>
  <p class="chapter-keyword">अनुष्टुभ् (Anuṣṭubh) – 8 syllables per quarter</p>
  <p class="chapter-theme"><strong>Theme:</strong> In an unmanaged common resource, individual rational profit-maximization guarantees collective ruin. The ego-deluded mind (अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा) externalizes costs onto the commons. The only remedy is mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon.<sup>1</sup></p>
</aside>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 5</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v5">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">साधारणे हि क्षेत्रेऽस्मिन् स्वार्थलाभो विनाशकः ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा सर्वनाशं नियच्छति ॥ ५ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">In this common field, the pursuit of self-interest is destructive. The ego-deluded mind brings about total ruin.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** साधारणे हि क्षेत्रे अस्मिन् स्व-अर्थ-लाभः विनाशकः । अहङ्कार-विमूढ-आत्मा सर्व-नाशं नियच्छति ॥

**अन्वयः:** अस्मिन् साधारणे क्षेत्रे हि स्वार्थलाभः विनाशकः (भवति)। अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा सर्वनाशं नियच्छति ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **साधारणे क्षेत्रे** – in the common field/resource. समानं धार्यते यत् तत् साधारणम् (common property). तस्मिन् क्षेत्रे (सप्तमी).
- **स्वार्थलाभः** – pursuit of self-interest / individual profit. स्वस्य अर्थः (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष) = स्वार्थः. स्वार्थस्य लाभः (षष्ठी तत्पुरुष).
- **विनाशकः** – destructive.
- **अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा** – one whose mind is deluded by ego. अहङ्कारेण विमूढः (तृतीया तत्पुरुष), विमूढः आत्मा यस्य सः (बहुव्रीहि). Echoes Bhagavad Gita 3.27, repurposing theological language for ecological diagnosis.
- **सर्वनाशम्** – total ruin / tragedy.
- **नियच्छति** – brings about / guarantees. नि + यम् + लट्.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>पूर्वपक्षः · सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पूर्वपक्षः:** Adam Smith's "invisible hand" suggests that individuals pursuing self-interest inevitably promote the good of society. This has been widely misinterpreted to mean that unmanaged exploitation of natural resources will self-correct through market forces.<sup>1</sup>

**सिद्धान्तः:** The direct Sanskrit codification of "The Tragedy of the Commons."<sup>1</sup> When a pasture, ocean or atmosphere is held in common (साधारणे क्षेत्रे) and is completely unmanaged, each individual user is rationally compelled to exploit it maximally. The individual receives 100 percent of the profit (स्वार्थलाभः) from adding a unit of exploitation, while the ecological cost is distributed fractionally among all users. Because this calculus applies to every user, the shared field is inevitably destroyed.<sup>1</sup> The text diagnoses this fatal rationality as a spiritual and systemic delusion (अहङ्कारविमूढात्मा), wherein the ego's failure to recognize the interconnectedness of the ecosystem ensures total collective ruin (सर्वनाशम्).<sup>1</sup>

</div>
</details>

<p class="verse-topic">अनुष्टुभ् 6</p>

<div class="sanskrit-text sanskrit-verse-lines" data-verse-id="v6">
  <span data-line="1" data-start="" data-end="">परस्परं नियमनं विना नास्ति हि रक्षणम् ।</span><br />
  <span data-line="2" data-start="" data-end="">स्वातन्त्र्यं यत्र सम्पूर्णं तत्र मृत्युर्ध्रुवो भवेत् ॥ ६ ॥</span>
</div>

<p class="prose-text">Without mutual regulation, there is no conservation. Where freedom is absolute, there death would be certain.</p>

<summary><strong>पदच्छेदः · अन्वयः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

**पदच्छेदः:** परस्परं नियमनं विना न अस्ति हि रक्षणम् । स्वातन्त्र्यं यत्र सम्पूर्णं तत्र मृत्युः ध्रुवः भवेत् ॥

**अन्वयः:** परस्परं नियमनं विना रक्षणम् हि न अस्ति। यत्र सम्पूर्णं स्वातन्त्र्यं (अस्ति), तत्र मृत्युः ध्रुवः भवेत् ॥

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **परस्परम्** – mutual. परश्च परश्च (avyayībhāva / karmavyatihāra).
- **नियमनम्** – coercion / regulation. नि + यम् + ल्युट्.
- **रक्षणम्** – protection / conservation.
- **स्वातन्त्र्यम्** – freedom. स्वतन्त्र + ष्यञ्. Abstract noun.
- **सम्पूर्णम्** – total / absolute.
- **मृत्युर्ध्रुवः** – death is certain. मृत्युः + ध्रुवः (विसर्गसन्धि – रुत्वम्, उत्वम्, गुणः).
- **भवेत्** – would be.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

The verse resolves the Tragedy of the Commons by codifying हारडिन्'s solution: "Mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon" (परस्परं नियमनम्).<sup>1</sup> Without collective, democratic restrictions on individual behavior, conservation (रक्षणम्) of the biosphere is impossible. It challenges the philosophical premise that absolute freedom is a supreme good. In a finite, crowded world, total freedom (सम्पूर्णं स्वातन्त्र्यम्) to breed or to pollute does not lead to utopia but guarantees systemic death (मृत्युर्ध्रुवः). Real freedom requires the recognition of necessity and the voluntary surrender of certain unrestricted liberties to preserve the whole.<sup>1</sup>

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>शब्दार्थाः · व्याकरणम्</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

- **प्रजा** – population.
- **कृषिः** – agriculture / food production.
- **यन्त्रम्** – machines / industrial output. यम् + त्रन्. Metonymy for industrial capital.
- **अनवीकरणीयिका** – non-renewable (resources). न नवः (अनवः); नवीकरणम् (च्वि प्रत्यय); अनवीकरणाय योग्या (अनीयर प्रत्यय); टाप् + कन्. 7 syllables (a-na-vī-ka-ra-ṇī-yi-kā), fitting the line end.
- **प्रदूषणम्** – pollution.
- **वर्धन्ते** – grow / increase.
- **नाशयन्ति** – destroy / cause collapse. नश् + णिच् (causative) + लट् प्रथमपुरुष बहुवचन.

<details>
<summary><strong>सिद्धान्तः</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

The verse codifies the five macroscopic variables of the World3 computer model from the 1972 *Limits to Growth* report.<sup>2</sup> The five factors – population (प्रजा), agricultural production (कृषिः), industrial output (यन्त्रम्), nonrenewable resource depletion (अनवीकरणीयिका) and pollution (प्रदूषणम्)<sup>6</sup> – interact through complex feedback loops. As they grow exponentially (वर्धन्ते), they ultimately drive overshoot and sudden, uncontrollable collapse of both population and industrial capacity (नाशयन्ति) within a finite planetary system.<sup>2</sup>

The legacy of the *Limits to Growth* paradigm has been vindicated by empirical observations tracked through the Planetary Boundaries framework by the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research and the Stockholm Resilience Centre.<sup>3</sup>

</details>

The ocean (जलनिधौ), acting as Earth's stabilizer, is rapidly transforming into a toxic acidic environment (अम्लपङ्कः) due to anthropogenic CO₂ absorption.<sup>3</sup> Surface ocean pH has dropped by 0.1 units since the industrial era, representing a 30–40% increase in acidity.<sup>3</sup> This transgression pushes marine ecosystems (cold-water corals, pteropods) beyond safe limits, confirming the trajectory of ecological collapse warned of by the original MIT models.<sup>2</sup>

<details>
<summary><strong>Linguistic and Philosophical Synthesis</strong></summary>
<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

Codifying modern ecological economics into classical Sanskrit requires a philosophical reconciliation. Sanskrit is a language engineered around the concept of *Ṛta* (cosmic order and balance) and *Dharma* (sustaining duty).<sup>13</sup> The modern environmental crisis, as diagnosed by Hardinian economics and the World3 computer models, is fundamentally a crisis of *Adharma* – a failure to sustain the balance between human extraction and planetary regeneration.

**Etymological Resonances:** The modern term "ecology," coined from the Greek *oikos* (household), shares semantic DNA with पारिस्थिनिकी.<sup>9</sup> Derived from परि (around/environment) and स्था (to stand/exist), it denotes the science of how entities stand in relation to their surroundings. When Hardinian theory attacks Cowboy Economics – the belief in infinite frontiers – it attacks the delusion that an entity can exist independently of its environment.<sup>1</sup>

The concept of "exponential growth" is codified as गुणोत्तरवृद्धि.<sup>14</sup> In classical Sanskrit mathematics and prosody, *vṛddhi* implies augmentation.<sup>14</sup> However, *guṇottara* specifies a geometric multiplier. The tragedy of the modern economic paradigm is the elevation of *vṛddhi* to an absolute good, ignoring the constraints of the *Brahmaṇḍa* (the bounded sphere of the universe). The codification makes explicitly clear that growth without limits is a biological and physical anomaly.<sup>1</sup>

**The Convergence of अहङ्कार and the Commons:** The systemic ruin of shared resources is driven by the rational pursuit of self-interest.<sup>1</sup> In classical philosophical systems, this drive is the direct manifestation of अहङ्कार – the ego or the principle of individuation that separates the "self" from the "whole."<sup>10</sup>

When the herdsman in Hardin's commons adds an extra animal to the pasture, he operates purely from ego, internalizing the profit and externalizing the ecological degradation.<sup>1</sup> The Sanskrit codification (*ahaṃkāravimūḍhātmā sarvanāśaṃ niyacchati*) elevates this economic critique to a spiritual diagnosis: the ego-driven mind, deluded by its perceived separation from nature, guarantees the destruction of the very substrate that supports its existence. The prescribed cure – mutual coercion and regulation (*parasparaṃ niyamanaṃ*) – functions as the societal enforcement of duty and interconnectedness over isolated self-interest.<sup>13</sup>

By utilizing the morphological precision of Pāṇinian grammar, concepts such as carrying capacity, exponential growth, planetary boundaries and systemic overshoot are mapped into a structurally rigorous format. The resulting text provides a timeless linguistic vessel for the most critical existential warnings of the modern era. Through this synthesis, the empirical data of the *Limits to Growth* and the 2025 Planetary Health Check are elevated from transient statistical reports into enduring philosophical dictums, reinforcing the reality that human survival depends entirely on respecting the finite boundaries of the natural world.

<div class="prose-text" markdown="1">

### What this post is about
**Load shedding** deliberately drops or degrades work under overload so admitted work still finishes inside budget. It is not backpressure (which slows senders) and not a steady rate limit (which holds a quota).

### Topics
1. Overload without total slowdown  
2. Early edge reject  
3. Priority classes  
4. Saturation signals  
5. Honest refuse and retry hint  
6. Client backoff contract  
7. Brownout before blackout  
8. Per-tenant fairness  
9. Game-day rehearsal  
10. Backpressure versus rate limit versus shed  
11. Five limbs  
12. Close  

English minimizable. Pure padya.

### ग्राह्यम् / Keep
१. **reject early**: before deep queues  
२. **protect critical classes**: drop batch first  
३. **trigger on saturation**: queue age and concurrency  
४. **return a cheap refuse**: with a retry hint  
५. **clients backoff**: no stampede  
६. **brownout optional features**: before core blackout  
७. **fair per tenant**: noisy neighbor first  

### त्याज्यम् / Avoid
- accept everything until a total timeout storm  
- silent hang instead of a clear refuse  
- global random drop while one tenant floods

अतिभारे सर्वं चेत् स्वीकृतं सर्वं मन्दं भवेत् ।

**Word-for-word**

| संस्कृतपदम् | Word-for-word English |
| :---- | :---- |

| सर्वम् | all |

**Gloss table**

If everything is accepted under overload, everything becomes slow and then everything times out. Shed so admitted work still succeeds. A queue that grows without a drop policy is a latency bomb with a dashboard.


**Context / topic**
